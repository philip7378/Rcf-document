Section 3: From 1D Existence to 3D Link Connectivity — Graph-Valence
Obstructions, Dynamical Vertex Coupling, and the Corner Link Frame

Theorem II.1 (Sec. 2) derived local directional dimension D_loc = 3
and SU(2) from complex associative rank-2 Jordan closure, feeding
directly into Paper I Theorem 1 to select S = 1 (C^3) as the unique
frustration-free single-violation-sector spin. This section
determines how multi-site spin-1 structures can extend beyond Paper
I's 1D open chain without violating the S = 1 bond condition — and
proves by two new No-Go theorems that static existence constraints
cannot branch into 3D on their own; cross-axis connectivity is
forced into the dynamical Lie and link sectors.

The architectural inversion relative to AKLT deserves stating once,
plainly, because it organizes everything below. In the AKLT
construction the graph is an input: the Hilbert space factorizes
over vertices, bonds are entangling edges drawn between them, and
the emergent dimension is the Hausdorff dimension of whatever graph
was put in. In the present framework the link is primitive and
the vertex is derived: D_loc = 3 counts independent 1D displacement
directions that close in the link algebra (Theorem II.1), and a
"vertex" is a relational locus — a composite register wired inside
the link-derived dimension, not a node that pre-exists it. The
No-Gos below are then exactly what this inversion predicts: a
vertex-first static overload of the spin-1 bond runs into the
valence wall (No-Go VIII), a static bulk vertex constraint kills
the very edge kernel it is supposed to knit (No-Go VIIIb), and only
the dynamical coupling H_vtx — the vertex as an active relational
object, not as geometry — carries cross-axis information (Sec. 3.3).
The corner link frame of Sec. 3.4 is the positive witness of the
inverted order: three intact links determine a rank-3 frame in
which the register sits as the incenter of the three stub
directions, and the junction's multiplicativity gap (Sec. 3.4.2) is
precisely the angle data the additive 1D logs cannot see.

3.1 No-Go VIII: The Bare Spin-1 Graph-Valence Obstruction [E4]

P_{ij}^{(2)} = (1/6)(S_i.S_j + 1)(S_i.S_j + 2).

3.4.2 The Junction Multiplicativity Gap and the Distance
Commitment (PIN 1, renamed per r7.0).
Along any single 1D AKLT axis, the static kernel correlation
(-1)^n (4/3^{n+1}) (base 1/3; kept distinct per Gate O / T4 from
Paper I Theorem 8's 2/3 dipole extension eigen-rate) yields exact
additive 1D log-distance. Across the 3-axis corner junction, the
raw pairwise -ln|K| produces an exact C_3-symmetric gap on all
three stub pairs:

    M(A, C; O) := d(A, C) - [d(A, O) + d(O, C)] = +1.168796,
    d = -ln|K|, O = barycenter anchor

(equivalently the K-space log-ratio
ln[K(A,C)/(K(A,O)K(O,C)] = -1.168796; direct stub-stub separation
4.4111 vs. via-center 3.2423; on the bare uncoupled kernel
K(e_mu, e_nu) = 0 and the object degenerates; on product seeds the
marginals vanish and the gap object is vacuous — r70_metric2
GUARD). The gap is a CORRELATION quantity, not a metric defect: it
is the amount by which the pair correlation across the junction
falls below the product-of-marginals, zero on product states,
nonzero exactly when the junction entangles. Reading it as a
"distance defect" was the category error: raw pairwise -ln|K| is a
link-level separation and a direct-pair diagnostic, not a
pseudometric on 3D frames.

Spatial link distance in Paper II is therefore committed to the
unconditional path-infimum d_int (Theorem S2, Sec. 5), with raw
-ln|K| retained only as the direct-pair diagnostic. On the
certified 6-node frame (3 registers, 3 stubs, edge weights
-ln|corr| from K_32), d_int(stub, stub) = 3.622512 on all three
pairs (C_3-symmetric).
