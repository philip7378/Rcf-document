# RCF: Legacy-to-Current Migration Map

### Porting Gen-2 rigor into the SOE/MOE foundation, section by section

Your instinct was right. I went back into `Glm.pdf`, `Con.pdf`, and `Ddd.pdf` (the most developed Gen‑2 manuscripts) and lined their formal machinery up against the current SOE/MOE standalone sections line by line. Below are **five concrete, verified places** where Gen 2 already built something Gen 3 needs and currently either lacks or only asserts. For each: what the old draft has, exactly where Gen 3 is thin at the equivalent spot, and the specific translation needed to bring it across.

I did the deepest excavation on Sections 0, 1, 2, 4, and 5, since those are foundational and the payoff is highest. Sections 3, 6, 7, 8, 9 are flagged at the end with a lighter first pass — say the word and I'll do the same surgical pass on them.

## 1. Section 0 — the "Strength Ladder" (Gen 2 has it; Gen 3 needs it)

**Where it lives:** `Glm.pdf` §0.4.5 (also in `Con.pdf`, `Ddd.pdf`, `RCF_n.pdf`)
**Gen‑3 gap:** standalone `Section_0…_1/_2.pdf`, Definition 0.1.3 (Master Constraint) → Lemma 0.2.1 (Kernel Containment) → Definition 0.6.1 (`A_phy`)

Gen 2 spent real effort proving that four conditions on a state ω are **not equivalent**, and got it wrong once before fixing it (that's what "Theorem — Constraint Inclusion (**Corrected**)" documents):

1. **Primitive quadratic vanishing:** ω(Ĉ_α†Ĉ_α)=0 for each constraint individually.
2. **Linear ideal annihilation:** ω(X)=0 for every X in the ideal 𝓘_C generated by the constraints — *this follows from (1)*.
3. **Ideal-null physicality:** ω(X†X)=0 for every X∈𝓘_C — a *strictly stronger* condition.
4. **GNS ideal inclusion:** 𝓘_C ⊆ 𝒩_ω (the ideal is null in the GNS representation) — equivalent to (3).

The proof that (1) does **not** imply (3) is the crux: an ideal element X = ΣA_iĈ_{α_i}B_i has X†X full of cross-terms like B†Ĉ†A†A'Ĉ'B', and primitive vanishing alone doesn't control those without extra structure. Con/Ddd/Glm all found this had been silently assumed in an earlier pass and now require ideal-null physicality as an **explicit fourth condition**, with three named special cases where it comes for free (mutually-commuting-mod-ideal constraints; irreducible GNS reps; KMS/clustering states).

**Why Gen 3 needs this:** Gen 3's Section 0 defines the physical sector via `Definition 0.6.1: A_phy = {A ∈ 𝒜 : π_kin(A) ker(M̂) ⊆ ker(M̂)}` — calling this a **sub-algebra** presupposes it's closed under multiplication and adjoint, which is exactly the question the Strength Ladder answers. The only thing Gen 3 currently proves is `Lemma 0.2.1: ker(M̂) ⊂ ker(Δ_αβ)` — containment for the *commutators*, not the ideal-null property needed to know `ker(M̂)` is actually invariant under left multiplication by the Ĉ_α themselves. That's a different, weaker statement standing in the same spot.

**Port path:**
- Add a `𝓘_C = ⟨Ĉ_α⟩` definition right after Def 0.1.2 (Primitive Constraints) — pure addition, doesn't disturb anything.
- Restate the four-condition ladder with Gen 3's own symbols: Ĉ_α unchanged, ω_kin in place of ω, ker(M̂) in place of 𝒩_ω.
- Check the same three "ideal-null is automatic" special cases against the SOE/MOE setup specifically — the SOE flow's claim that it "never erases phase, never suppresses coherences" (§0.4.1) plausibly gives you something like case 1 or 3 for free, but that link isn't made explicitly anywhere in the current draft and would be worth stating outright rather than leaving implicit.
- This closes a real hole: right now `A_phy` being a genuine algebra is *assumed* by calling it one, not shown.

## 2. Section 0 — the fully-worked toy model

**Where it lives:** `Glm.pdf` §0.6 (also Con/Ddd/RCF_n, with minor variations)
**Gen‑3 gap:** standalone Section 0's toy model is one paragraph: *"On M_6(ℂ) with non-commuting constraints, starting from Tr(ρ_0M)=0.96, the flow drives Tr(ρM) to 2.14e-4 and ker(M) support from 7.6% to 99.8%."* Numbers only, no construction shown.

Gen 2's version builds 𝒜 = M_2(ℂ)⊕M_2(ℂ) explicitly (a "system qubit" ⊕ "constraint qubit"), defines Ĉ=(0,P_1), computes the ideal 𝓘_C={0}⊕M_2(ℂ) by hand, exhibits an explicit physical state ω(A_S,A_C)=⟨0|A_S|0⟩, verifies positivity/normalization/ideal-nullity by direct calculation, constructs the 2-dimensional GNS Hilbert space and cyclic vector explicitly, and then — this is the useful part — **carries the same toy model into the correlation kernel**, computing K_ω(σ_x,σ_y)=1, K_ω(σ_x,σ_z)=0 by hand with real Pauli-matrix algebra, and showing the resulting emergent point set has exactly two points at infinite separation.

**Port path:** this is close to a direct port. Gen 3's algebra is simpler (a single kinematic algebra with a master constraint, no Tier/ideal split), so the toy model would need one adaptation: pick a small 𝒜 with a genuinely non-commuting constraint pair (Gen 3's own M_6(ℂ) example already has this), then walk through *the same six steps Gen 2 does* — explicit ω_kin, explicit F̂ and its spectral decomposition, explicit ker(M̂) construction, explicit SOE/MOE flow applied by hand for a few steps, explicit K_ω calculation. The scaffolding is reusable even though the underlying algebra differs; what's missing in Gen 3 is the discipline of showing the arithmetic, not the setup.

## 3. Section 1 — the strict-partial-order proof, and a likely missing assumption

**Where it lives:** `Glm.pdf` §1.3.5–1.3.9
**Gen‑3 gap:** standalone `Section_1`, Theorem 1.2.1 ("Causal Partial Order") — stated, tagged `[Established Theorem]`, **no proof given** in any of the three drafts (unnumbered, `_1`, `_2`/`_3`).

Gen 2 defines precedence via constraint-closure inclusion: e_i≺e_j ⟺ Γ(e_i)⊊Γ(e_j) *and* Γ(e_i) is necessary for Γ(e_j). Irreflexivity and asymmetry fall out of set-inclusion being irreflexive/asymmetric — easy. Transitivity is the interesting part: Gen 2 is honest that it **doesn't come for free** — it needs an extra, explicitly named `Assumption 1.1 (Necessity Composition)`: that "necessary for" composes across a chain, defended by analogy to transitivity of logical entailment.

**My own observation, not something the documents state explicitly — worth checking rather than taken as fact:** Gen 3's definition of ≺ is `d(E_1) < d(E_2) AND π_kin(E_1)π_kin(E_2)|ψ⟩ ≠ 0 for some ψ`. Because the first conjunct is a strict inequality between real numbers, **irreflexivity and asymmetry come for free** automatically (you can't have d(E_1)<d(E_2) and d(E_2)<d(E_1) at once) — that's actually a small win over Gen 2's more laborious closure-based route. But **transitivity of the compound relation still isn't free**: even though d(E_1)<d(E_2)<d(E_3) gives d(E_1)<d(E_3) automatically, there's no general reason the "relational connectivity" clause (π_kin(E_1)π_kin(E_2)|ψ⟩≠0 and π_kin(E_2)π_kin(E_3)|ψ'⟩≠0) should force π_kin(E_1)π_kin(E_3)|ψ''⟩≠0 for *some* ψ'' — operator non-vanishing doesn't compose like that in general.

**Port path:** Gen 3 likely needs its own version of Gen 2's Assumption 1.1 — something like "connectivity composes across a causal chain within a sector" — stated as an explicit assumption rather than left implicit inside an "Established Theorem" tag. Given Gen 3's depth-based definition already does half the work for free, this would actually be a *shorter* proof than Gen 2's once the one missing assumption is named.

## 4. Section 1/2 — Emergent Direction, isotropy, and the mass-shell relation (missing wholesale)

**Where it lives:** `Glm.pdf` §1.7 (also Con/Ddd)
**Gen‑3 gap:** nothing corresponding exists anywhere in the standalone Section 1 or Section 2 drafts.

This is the apparatus that gets you from "there's a finite speed limit" to "the geometry is actually Lorentzian," which are not the same claim (an anisotropic medium can have a finite max speed with no Lorentz symmetry at all). Gen 2 builds it in four steps:
- **Distance profiles** Φ_A(X):=d_ω(A,X) and **displacement profiles** Δ_{A→B}(X):=Φ_B(X)−Φ_A(X), proven antisymmetric and vanishing on the diagonal.
- **Direction equivalence** (profiles agreeing up to positive rescaling) and an **emergent tangent cone** T_A — the first pre-vectorial notion of direction, built with no manifold assumed.
- **Theorem — Direction Requires Distinguishability**: if A and B collapse to the same emergent point (d_ω(A,B)=0), no direction exists between them — a clean two-line proof from the triangle inequality.
- **Directional isotropy** as a named, flagged *regime assumption* (not proven, explicitly marked as such) → under isotropy, a **Conditional Proposition** gets you the Lorentz-form interval ds²=−c_*²dτ²+dℓ_ω², and from there an **emergent mass-shell relation** p_μp^μ=−m_eff²c_*².

Gen 2 is careful about status here — three explicit tiers: speed bound is a conditional theorem, isotropy is a regime assumption, full Lorentz symmetry is an effective continuum theorem target. That epistemic discipline is worth copying along with the math.

**Port path:** Section 2's `d_ω` and `K_ω` carry over with zero change (same symbols, same definitions in both generations at this point). The direction/tangent-cone apparatus sits directly on top and doesn't reference anything Tier/Ladder-specific — it's a clean, near-direct port. The mass-shell relation is the more valuable end point: Gen 3 currently has no result connecting its burden-derived mass (m=λ_min(F̂), Section 4) to a relativistic dispersion relation at all — this would supply it.

## 5. Section 2 — the pseudometric proof, and a second, independent D=3 argument

**Where it lives:** `Glm.pdf` §2.3 (pseudometric) and §2.6 (dimensional closure)
**Gen‑3 gap:** `Section_2..._2.pdf`, Theorem 2.2.1 (asserted, not proved) and Theorem 2.3.3 (`[Theorem Target]`, explicitly unproven)

**(a) Pseudometric proof.** Gen 2 states the needed kernel property up front as **Assumption 2.5 (Multiplicative Correlation Consistency)**: K_ω(A,C) ≥ K_ω(A,B)K_ω(B,C), then gives a clean four-line proof that taking −ℓ_0·log of a multiplicative inequality flips it into the additive triangle inequality. It's also explicit that you only get a *pseudometric*, not a metric, without a further separation assumption (K_ω=1 ⟹ A=B). Gen 3's Section 2 has the identical `d_ω = −ℓ_0 log|K_ω|` definition and asserts the pseudometric properties as a bare `Theorem 2.2.1` with no proof and no named assumption doing the work. **Direct port** — same symbols, same ℓ_0, same K_ω; only the phase-preserving `|K_ω|` needs carrying through the log step, which is routine since the modulus is what's bounded in (0,1].

**(b) A second D=3 argument.** This is the more interesting one. Gen 3's D=3 result rests entirely on cubic-kernel non-degeneracy (Gram determinant of the phase-preserving cubic kernel), and is honestly tagged `[Theorem Target]` — the genericity of that non-degeneracy is unproven. Gen 2 has a **completely different, independently-argued route** to the same conclusion: a "relational inference channel counting" argument. Existence (binary), position (scalar, from d_ω), and direction (vectorial, from the displacement profile in #4 above) are argued to be three algebraically incommensurable inference channels that cannot be losslessly compressed into fewer dimensions (**Assumption 2.7**), giving a closure-defect function Ξ_C(D) that is provably positive for D≠3 and zero at D=3 — a genuine proof-by-cases, conditional on Assumption 2.7.

**Port path — and an honest caveat:** this doesn't replace Gen 3's cubic-kernel route, it's a second, independent argument for the same conclusion, and it carries its own unproven assumption (2.7) rather than being unconditionally stronger. But two independent conditional arguments for D=3 — one algebraic (Gram determinant), one information/inference-theoretic (channel counting) — is meaningfully more persuasive than either alone, and costs little to include: "existence/position/direction" map directly onto Gen 3's own vocabulary (position = d_ω, direction = the Section 1/2 direction apparatus in #4), so this could be added as `2.3.x — An Independent Argument for D=3` without touching anything else in the section.

## 6. Section 4 — matter/antimatter from positivity (cheapest high-value port in the whole set)

**Where it lives:** `Con.pdf` §4.3 **only** — confirmed absent from Ddd, RCF_n, and Glm, and absent from all three standalone Section 4 drafts.

Con's Theorem 4.2 (Positivity as Matter-Antimatter Bias) makes a clean argument: the orientation invariant O_ω(A,B,C)=Im[K̃_ω(A,B)K̃_ω(B,C)K̃_ω(C,A)] already splits triads into right-handed (O_ω>0, "matter") and left-handed (O_ω<0, "antimatter") sectors. If the universe were matter-antimatter symmetric, ω could be an indefinite functional where the two sectors cancel — but the **strict positivity postulate** ω(A†A)≥0, already foundational in every generation of this framework, structurally forbids that cancellation. The GNS quotient keeps only a positive-definite sector, so positivity itself is the origin of the asymmetry, with antimatter surviving only as the adjoint of a matter mode rather than as an independent negative sector.

**Port path — near-zero effort.** Positivity of states is common to Gen 2 and Gen 3 (it's baked into the definition of "kinematic state" in Gen 3's own Section 0.1.4). The orientation invariant O_ω already exists in the current standalone Section 2 (§2.3.4) and Section 4 (§4.5.1, "Chirality from SOE Invariant") — Gen 3 already uses O_ω for the spin-statistics conjecture but never draws Con's positivity connection. This is close to a copy-paste: rename 𝒱_ω→V_ω / K̃_ω→K_ω to match current notation, cite the SOE-invariance of O_ω (already proven in `_2`/`_3`) in place of Con's plainer chirality definition, and the theorem goes through essentially unchanged.

## 7. Section 4 — the machinery behind the Complexity-Symmetry Correspondence

**Where it lives:** `Con.pdf` §4.5 / `Ddd.pdf` §4.4 (absent from RCF_n and Glm, which drop this in favor of "many-body generalisation / locality / interaction vertices" instead)
**Gen‑3 gap:** standalone `Section_4..._2.pdf`, Conjecture 4.3.4 — states the conclusion only: *"U(1) corresponds to κ=1 … SU(2) corresponds to κ=2 … SU(3) corresponds to κ=3 … [Structural Conjecture] — quarantined. The specific group assignment is not yet derived."*

This is the cleanest case in the whole review of a later draft keeping a conjecture's **headline** while dropping the **argument** that made it a real (if unproven) conjecture rather than a bare assertion. Con/Ddd actually build it:
- **Definition 4.8 (Internal Complexity)** κ(M): the minimal internal-channel dimension needed to sustain a stable mode.
- The symmetry group is *defined* as the automorphism group of the internal burden functional: G = {U ∈ GL(κ,ℂ) : B_int(UΦ)=B_int(Φ)} — and the κ=1→U(1), κ=2→SU(2), κ=3→SU(3) correspondence is argued from what breaks the full U(κ) down to a subgroup once non-additive (cubic-kernel) coupling enters.
- **The Sub-Ideal Decomposition**: 𝓘_C = 𝓘_C^(1)⊕𝓘_C^(2)⊕… — an actual proposed algebraic *origin* for why a mode would have κ>1 internal channels at all, tied to the constraint ideal splitting into orthogonal pieces, each generating its own burden-flux channel and making the covariant Laplacian non-Abelian.

**Port path:** Gen 3 already has the ingredients this needs — internal reconciliation structure (Def 4.3.1), the automorphism group G=Aut(R_int) (Theorem 4.3.2), and a constraint ideal implicitly present in the M̂ construction. What's missing is Def 4.8's explicit κ and the Sub-Ideal Decomposition connecting κ to the algebra. Translating 𝓘_C→ the ideal generated by {Ĉ_α} in Gen 3's own Section 0 notation, and B_int → an SOE-flux-restricted burden functional, would upgrade this from an asserted label-matching into the same kind of structural (if still quarantined) conjecture Con actually argued for.

## 8. Section 5 — the Lorentzian-signature proof (and a heads-up: Glm is *not* the best source here)

**Where it lives:** `Con.pdf` §5.3.2 and `Ddd.pdf` §5.3.2 (**"Theorem — Emergent Lorentzian Signature"**)
**Important correction to my first report:** I recommended `Glm.pdf` as the generally-strongest Gen‑2 manuscript, and for most of the framework that holds. **This is the one place it doesn't.** Glm's equivalent theorem is retitled "Effective Metric Ansatz" and its entire proof is one line — *"By substituting the burden definitions… we obtain the effective burden metric"* — which is a relabeling, not a derivation. Con and Ddd both keep the real argument; Glm quietly dropped it.

Con's actual proof: given the causal speed bound (d_ℓω ≤ c_eff·dτ for causally connected pairs), write the general interval ds²=s·c_eff²dτ²+h^(B)_ij dx^i dx^j with sign s=±1 undetermined, then check both regimes — **timelike** (causally connected, needs ds²≤0 to mark it as timelike) forces s=−1; **spacelike** (causally incomparable, no bound applies, needs ds²>0) *also* forces s=−1. Both cases independently land on the same sign, which is what "forced, not assumed" is supposed to mean.

**Why Gen 3 needs this specifically:** Gen 3's own Section 2 Type-Sign Coupling theorem (§2.4.2) says the minus sign "can only attach to the scalar sector" but explicitly flags: *"This is a necessary condition; the derivation of why a minus sign exists at all remains a Theorem Target."* Con's proof is a direct, ready-to-adapt candidate for closing exactly that named gap.

**Port path:** the causal speed bound already exists in Gen 3 (Section 1, §1.3, the SOE propagation-speed theorem c=γ·ℓ_0), and the timelike/spacelike split is exactly what Gen 3's causal order ≺ (Section 1) vs. antichain structure already distinguishes. Con's case-split argument transplants with almost no notation change — c_eff→c=γℓ_0, everything else already matches.
